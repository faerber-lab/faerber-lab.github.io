LLM Routing

Open
Subhendu Khatuya subhendu.khatuya@tu-dresden.de 2 students

With the proliferation of Specialized Large Language Models (LLMs) varying in size, cost, and capability, dynamically directing user queries to the most suitable model is critical. This project focuses on developing/evaluating lightweight routing mechanisms (e.g., using small classifier models, embedding similarity, or performance predictors) to automatically route incoming prompts to the optimal LLM (e.g., smaller/faster model for simple queries vs. larger model for complex tasks). The goal is to maximize overall response quality while minimizing computational cost and response latency.

Mitigating Medium-Novelty Bias in Research Idea Judgment

Open
Tobias Schreieder tobias.schreieder@tu-dresden.de 1 student

Large language models (LLMs) can assess the novelty of research ideas, but existing approaches exhibit a systematic medium-novelty bias: predictions tend to concentrate on intermediate novelty scores, while very low- and high-novelty ideas are harder to identify. The RINoBench benchmark enables systematic evaluation of this behavior, while the recent Think-Probe-Respond (TPR) approach demonstrates that classifiers operating on internal LLM representations can mitigate this bias.

Description

Large language models (LLMs) can assess the novelty of research ideas, but existing approaches exhibit a systematic medium-novelty bias: predictions tend to concentrate on intermediate novelty scores, while very low- and high-novelty ideas are harder to identify. The RINoBench benchmark enables systematic evaluation of this behavior, while the recent Think-Probe-Respond (TPR) approach demonstrates that classifiers operating on internal LLM representations can mitigate this bias.

This project investigates alternative classification approaches for mitigating medium-novelty bias in research idea judgment. Students will analyze relevant approaches for classification and judgment biases and investigate whether dedicated models or training strategies can improve the identification of low- and high-novelty research ideas. Possible approaches are not restricted to LLMs and may include encoder models, neural classifiers, or other suitable classification architectures.

Expected deliverables

  • Literature analysis: Review research on classification and judgment biases and identify promising mitigation approaches.
  • Baseline implementation: Implement and evaluate suitable RINoBench baselines.
  • Develop and implement at least one classification approach that targets a medium-novelty bias and is grounded in relevant literature.
  • Evaluation and analysis: Compare the proposed approach with the baselines using overall and class-specific metrics and analyze its effect on medium-novelty bias.
  • Scientific report: Document the project in the style of a scientific research paper. Ensure reproducibility of the experiments.

Prerequisites

  • Very good programming skills in Python
  • Strong background in machine learning
  • Good understanding of large language models
  • Experience with PyTorch and/or Hugging Face Transformers is beneficial

References

Open
Zhan Qu zhan.qu@tu-dresden.de 2 students

This thesis invites you to work at the intersection of machine learning, multimodal AI, and digital health. Most current models focus on a single modality – but real clinical understanding requires joint reasoning over all of them.

Description

This thesis invites you to work at the intersection of machine learning, multimodal AI, and digital health. Electronic Health Records (EHRs) increasingly combine several data types: structured tabular data (diagnoses, procedures, lab values), unstructured clinical text (reports, summaries), medical images (e.g., X-rays), and clinical time series (vital signs, monitoring data). Prerequisites

  • Familiarity with transformers or sequence models; basic NLP, time-series or computer vision knowledge

Weakly Supervised Citation Localization in Scientific Full Texts

Open
Jingbo He jingbo.he@tu-dresden.de 1 student

When a paper cites another work, it usually relies on one specific finding in the cited paper, but the citation graph only records that paper P cites paper B, not which passage of B is meant. Locating this passage is the first step towards checking whether cited sources actually support the claims made about them, a problem that has become pressing with LLM-assisted writing. Human-labelled data for this task is scarce, and current systems rely on generic retrieval. This project investigates whether the citation network itself can serve as training signal: unarXive provides millions of arXiv full texts with in-text citations linked to the cited papers, and papers that cite the same work for the same finding provide natural, if noisy, labels. Students will design a weak-supervision strategy for citation localization, train a retrieval model on it, and evaluate against standard baselines on existing annotated data. Suitable approaches include dense retrievers, rerankers, late-interaction models, and noise-aware training methods.

BabelDojo: Do Prompt-Injection Defenses for LLM Agents Survive Outside English?

Open
Himanshu Beniwal himanshu.beniwal@tu-dresden.de 2 students

Tool-using agents read emails, web pages and documents that an attacker can write. Instructions hidden in that data (indirect prompt injection) can hijack the agent into sending money or leaking files. Nearly every benchmark and defense has been tested only in English. You will extend the AgentDojo benchmark with injections in about 10 languages (high- to low-resource, plus code-switched and romanized text) and measure how attack success, task utility and defense performance change with the language of the attack.

Description

This project investigates how contextual information can improve LLM-based text simplification. Building on our work on Example-Guided Prompting (EGP), where retrieved simplification examples are used to guide generation, the project will study a broader range of context types and how they should be selected and presented to the model. Relevant context may include examples of previous simplifications, document-level information, target-reader characteristics, lexical and syntactic constraints, domain knowledge, and discourse context from surrounding passages. The goal is to determine which types of context improve simplicity while preserving meaning, factual consistency, and coherence.

The project can compare different context configurations and context-selection strategies, including fixed context, retrieval-based context, and dynamically selected context. It may also investigate whether too much or irrelevant context harms simplification, analogous to what we observed in context-aware code generation. Evaluation would combine standard simplification metrics such as SARI, LENS, BERTScore, and readability measures with factuality and human evaluation.

Possible research questions

  • Which types of context are most useful for LLM-based text simplification?
  • How should relevant context be selected and represented?
  • Does combining several context types improve simplification, or can excessive context be detrimental?
  • How does context affect the trade-off between simplicity, meaning preservation, factuality, and document-level coherence?
  • Can context-aware prompting outperform standard zero-shot prompting and EGP?

Expected deliverables

  • Literature review of text simplification with LLMs.
  • Case-study research paper (with different types of context).

Prerequisites

  • Background in NLP and LLMs
  • Experience with PyTorch and/or Hugging Face Transformers

Context-Aware Code Development with AI Assistance

Open
Marina Litvak litvak.marina@gmail.com 2 students

This project investigates how AI—particularly large language models (LLMs)—can assist software developers by generating and adapting code based on contextual information. While modern AI coding assistants can produce syntactically correct code, they often struggle to account for the broader context of a project, such as existing classes, dependencies, APIs, and architectural constraints.

Show details ▾Hide details ▴

Description

This project investigates how AI—particularly large language models (LLMs)—can assist software developers by generating and adapting code based on contextual information. While modern AI coding assistants can produce syntactically correct code, they often struggle to account for the broader context of a project, such as existing classes, dependencies, APIs, and architectural constraints.

The goal of this project is to design and evaluate a system that improves code generation by incorporating different types of context, including: local context (method or function description), structural context (file, class, and package structure), dependency context (APIs, libraries, imports), and semantic context (functional requirements and intent).

The student will implement a context-aware pipeline, potentially using retrieval-augmented generation (RAG), where relevant code snippets or project artifacts are retrieved and provided to the model during generation. The project will compare context-aware generation with standard prompt-based approaches to assess improvements in correctness, integration, and usability.

Possible research questions

  • How do different types of context affect code generation quality?
  • Does more context always help, or can it overload the model?
  • Which context (API, structure, semantics) is most useful?
  • Can retrieval improve reuse of existing code components?

Expected deliverables

  • Case-study research paper (with extensive experiments using LLMs and different types of context).

Prerequisites

  • Background in software engineering and LLMs (security is an advantage)
  • Experience with PyTorch and/or Hugging Face Transformers

How Close Are Information Retrieval (IR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP)? A Study of Top-Tier Conferences

Open
Marina Litvak litvak.marina@gmail.com 1 student

This project investigates the convergence between Information Retrieval (IR) and Natural Language Processing (NLP) by analyzing publications from leading conferences (e.g., SIGIR, WSDM, ACL, EMNLP) over the past decade. The goal is to quantify how the two fields have become increasingly interconnected through shared tasks, methods, and research communities. The study will examine topic overlap (e.g., question answering, retrieval-augmented generation), methodological trends (e.g., transformers, embeddings), and citation patterns between the communities. By combining bibliometric analysis with content-based clustering of papers, the project aims to provide empirical evidence of the growing integration of IR and NLP, particularly in the era of large language models.

Claim Detection in Sensitive and Multilingual Domains

Open
Marina Litvak litvak.marina@gmail.com 2 students

This project focuses on the automatic identification of verifiable, check-worthy claims in text — a foundational task in computational fact-checking and misinformation analysis. While claim detection has advanced significantly in English news and political discourse, it remains largely unexplored in sensitive domains (legal, clinical, financial) and low-resource languages.

Show details ▾Hide details ▴

Description

This project focuses on the automatic identification of verifiable, check-worthy claims in text — a foundational task in computational fact-checking and misinformation analysis. While claim detection has advanced significantly in English news and political discourse, it remains largely unexplored in sensitive domains (legal, clinical, financial) and low-resource languages.

A core challenge is that existing models treat claim detection as a simple binary classification task, ignoring the context in which a claim appears: who makes it, in what type of document, and for what audience. This project will investigate how contextual signals — document type, speaker role, domain ontology, and discourse structure — can improve both the accuracy and interpretability of claim detection models.

The student will survey existing datasets and benchmarks (e.g., ClaimBuster, CLEF CheckThat!), implement and compare baseline models ranging from fine-tuned transformer classifiers to LLM-based zero-shot approaches, and explore at least one of the research directions below.

Possible research questions

  • Domain adaptation: how well do models trained on political speech transfer to legal or clinical text?
  • Multilingual claim detection: developing or extending datasets for Hebrew or other low-resource languages.
  • Privacy-aware claim detection: does masking or replacing named entities (text anonymization) degrade a model's ability to identify and rank claims?
  • LLM-based detection: how well do instruction-tuned LLMs rank check-worthiness under zero-shot and few-shot prompting, and what are their failure modes (e.g., hallucination, inconsistency)?

Expected deliverables

  • Research paper

Prerequisites

  • Background in NLP and LLMs
  • Experience with PyTorch and/or Hugging Face Transformers

Phonetic-Aware Letter-Based Next Word Prediction for Transliterated South Asian Languages

Filled
A F M Mohimenul Joaa a_f_m_mohimenul.joaa@mailbox.tu-dresden.de 1 student

Over a billion South Asian users type their languages in Latin script on English keyboards, but autocomplete serves them poorly: suggestions only appear once a word is complete, valid spelling variants are treated as different words, and code-mixed sentences confuse single-language models. This project builds a single on-device model for Hindi-, Urdu-, Bangla- and Tamil-English transliterated typing that predicts after every keystroke, uses phonetic matching (weighted edit distance over sound and consonant patterns) to treat spelling variants as equivalent, and ranks top-K suggestions by context. Lightweight text commands (e.g. ?fix, ?formal, ?shorten, ?translate) extend it beyond autocomplete.

KGNode v2: Self-Tuning Agentic Framework for Universal Knowledge Graph Question Answering

Filled
A F M Mohimenul Joaa a_f_m_mohimenul.joaa@mailbox.tu-dresden.de 1 student

KGNode is a training-free knowledge-graph question answering pipeline evaluated on DBLP-QuAD and QALD-10. Version 2 turns it into a self-optimising agentic system: a LangGraph workflow with quality gates that stop early on poor intermediate results, DSPy-driven prompt optimisation for entity extraction, template generation and SPARQL synthesis, and automatic hyperparameter adaptation to the query and the KG. The system is packaged as an MCP server and evaluated against v1 on accuracy, runtime and cross-domain transfer, with ablations per component.
